Who We Are
A studio built at the intersection
of craft and place.
"The tools changed. The craft didn't."

Lucas & Liam Legacy Studios is a Cape Breton cinematic production studio founded by Lucas Cotton — filmmaker, storyteller, and father. We build brand films, heritage documentaries, and complete digital identities for tourism businesses and organizations across Atlantic Canada.

What makes us different isn't the tools. It's the place. Every frame we shoot comes from someone who grew up on this island — who knows what the fog off the Bras d'Or looks like at 6am, what a Cape Breton voice sounds like, and why the stories here matter. We don't parachute in. We're already here.

We work across three verticals that most studios treat as separate businesses: cinematic film production, web design and digital identity, and heritage documentary for broadcast. For our clients that means one relationship, one creative vision, and work that holds together across every screen it appears on.

Our documentary work airs on Telile Community Television. Our animated characters — Cod B'y and Puffy of the Atlantic Cinematic Universe — are built on primary source historical research. Our music releases under Rogue Tide on major streaming platforms. We've been featured on East Coast Rising through 101.5 The Hawk.

We work fast. Most projects film in a single day and deliver within two to three weeks. No account managers, no agency overhead — just Lucas on the phone and Lucas on location.

Media & Press
PRESS KIT

Lucas Cotton is a Cape Breton filmmaker, songwriter, and content creator operating out of St. Peter's, Nova Scotia. He is the founder of Lucas & Liam Legacy Studios — a cinematic production studio building original films, animated series, heritage documentaries, and music rooted in Atlantic Canadian place and culture.

His animated characters Cod B'y and Puffy anchor the Atlantic Cinematic Universe — a growing creative framework built on primary source Cape Breton history. His music releases under the name Rogue Tide on all major streaming platforms. His documentary work airs on Telile Community Television under a formal Letter of Support and broadcast agreement.

Most recently, Lucas produced "The Clapping Stopped" — an original song and fully animated Pixar-realism video created in solidarity with the 2,200 CUPE Nova Scotia long-term care workers on strike across 24 homes. The video was produced using Richmond Villa in St. Peter's as its primary setting.

All work is produced independently in Cape Breton. No agency. No middleman. Every story built to last.
